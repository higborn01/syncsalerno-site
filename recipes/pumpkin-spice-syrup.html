Pumpkin Spice Syrup

The first batch came out too dark and tasted more like pumpkin pie filling than a spice syrup. The fix: white sugar instead of brown, a fraction of the pumpkin, a strain step for the grit, and a cinnamon-forward blend with no ginger or allspice — those are what push a spice mix toward “pie.”

Ingredients

  • 2 1/3 cups granulated sugar (466 g)
  • 1/4 cup + 2 tsp pumpkin purée (65 g)
  • 1 3/4 cups water (414 g)
  • 3 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon (9 g)
  • 1/3 tsp ground nutmeg, scant (0.6 g)
  • 1/3 tsp ground clove, scant (0.7 g)
  • 2 1/3 tsp vanilla extract (12 g)
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk (397 g)
  • 1/4 tsp salt, scant (2 g)

Method

  1. Combine the sugar, pumpkin purée, water, cinnamon, nutmeg, clove, and salt in a saucepan.
  2. Simmer on medium-low, whisking, until the sugar dissolves — about 5–7 minutes. Don't let it reduce hard or caramelize; that's what darkens the color.
  3. Remove from heat and strain through a fine-mesh sieve or cheesecloth to catch the undissolved spice grit — this fixes the graininess in coffee.
  4. Off the heat, whisk in the condensed milk and vanilla until smooth. Taste before bottling — if it still reads as pie, cut the clove back.
  5. Cool, bottle, and refrigerate. Makes about 1,571 g, roughly four 14 oz cans worth.
