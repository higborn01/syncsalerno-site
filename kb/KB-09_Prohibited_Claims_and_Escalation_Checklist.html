KB-09: Prohibited Claims and Escalation Checklist

Catalog Copilot Knowledge Base | Team-authored | v1.0, September 2026

Purpose

This checklist is the part of Catalog Copilot that makes it more than a copywriting tool. The GPT runs every draft against it before a human sees the draft, and it either removes a problem claim, flags it, or sends it to a person who can decide. Every rule cites the primary source it comes from in KB-04 or KB-05, so a reviewer can check the reasoning instead of taking the GPT's word for it.

This checklist has not been reviewed by a lawyer. It is a conservative reading of the FTC and Google documents, written so the GPT errs toward escalation. When a claim is close, the answer is to escalate, not to decide.

How the Tiers Work

Tier GPT Action Who Decides
1: Never write Remove the claim from the draft and note what was removed No one; the claim does not appear. A reviewer can add it back only with documented substantiation and brand reviewer sign-off.
2: Escalate Keep the claim, mark it [ESCALATE: rule ID], and block approval Brand reviewer, after checking the substantiation file
3: Allowed with data Write it only if the attribute in the source data supports it Category merchandiser confirms during normal review

Tier 3: Allowed When the Data Supports It

Material, color, dimensions, weight, care instructions, oven or dishwasher compatibility, assembly requirements, and what is included in the box can all be written when the matching attribute is present in the source data (KB-02 or the merchandiser's input). The GPT should copy the attribute's meaning without upgrading it. "Solid wood legs" in the data does not become "solid wood construction" in the copy.

Notes on the Environmental Rules

The Green Guides are the rules most likely to trip up product copy, and they are also under FTC review, which opened in late 2022. Two points matter most for the GPT. First, the FTC treats unqualified general claims like "eco-friendly" as very hard to substantiate, which is why they are escalated rather than allowed. Second, "recyclable" has to be qualified if recycling facilities are not available to a substantial majority of consumers where the product is sold. Until the revised Guides are published, every environmental claim goes to a person.

Escalation Steps

  • Step 1. The GPT marks each Tier 2 claim inline, for example [ESCALATE: T2-01], and lists all flags at the top of the draft.

  • Step 2. The category merchandiser confirms the flag is real and attaches any substantiation (certifications, supplier documentation, test reports).

  • Step 3. The brand reviewer approves the claim as written, approves a qualified version, or removes it.

  • Step 4. The decision and the rule ID are recorded in the KB-11 decision log, so the next similar claim has a precedent to check.
