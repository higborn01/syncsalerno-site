KB-01 Brand Voice and Style Guide

KB-01: Brand Voice and Style Guide

Catalog Copilot Knowledge Base | Team-authored | v1.0, September 2026

What This Guide Is For

This guide is the standard Catalog Copilot writes against. Every draft the GPT produces should read like it came from the same person at our pilot retailer, whether it is describing a floor lamp or a leather belt. The retailer sells home goods, furniture, kitchen products, and apparel accessories to shoppers who mostly want to know three things: what the product is, whether it fits their space or their life, and whether they can trust what the page says. The voice is built around that last point. We would rather undersell a product than write a sentence a reviewer has to walk back.

This guide covers voice and format only. It does not decide what claims are allowed; that is KB-09. When the two seem to conflict, KB-09 wins.

1. Voice Principles

Plain over clever. Write the way a helpful store employee talks when a customer asks a direct question. No puns in titles, no wordplay that makes the shopper stop and decode the sentence.

Specific over impressive. "Holds a 13-inch laptop and a water bottle" is better than "spacious." If a number exists in the product attributes, use it instead of an adjective.

Benefit, then fact. Lead each bullet with what the feature does for the shopper, then back it with the attribute that proves it. The fact is what keeps the benefit honest.

Warm, not gushing. One piece of evaluative language per description is plenty. Exclamation points are not used at all.

Second person. Address the shopper as "you." Refer to the product by its type ("this lamp," "the chair"), not "our amazing lamp."

2. Audience Profiles

Category Typical Shopper What They Need From the Copy
Home goods Renters and first-time owners furnishing on a budget Size, material, care instructions, and whether it matches common decor
Furniture Buyers making a larger purchase who are worried about fit and assembly Exact dimensions, weight capacity, assembly time, and what is in the box
Kitchen Home cooks, from beginner to regular Capacity, heat and dishwasher compatibility, material that touches food
Apparel accessories Gift buyers and repeat customers Sizing, material, closure type, and how it wears over time

3. Reading Level and Length

  • Target a Flesch-Kincaid grade level of 8 or lower for bullets and the description paragraph.

  • Keep sentences under 25 words. Vary the length; a short sentence after a long one is fine.

  • Description paragraph: 50 to 120 words. Longer copy is only for furniture that needs assembly or sizing detail.

  • No more than one idea per bullet.

4. Format Standards

Titles

Order: Brand + Product Type + Key Attribute + Size or Color. Target 60 to 80 characters; never exceed 150 (the Google Merchant Center limit in KB-05). Use title case. No promotional text, no all caps, no symbols used for attention.

Example: "Fernhollow Ceramic Table Lamp with Linen Shade, 22 Inch, Sage Green"

Bullets

Three to five bullets. Each starts with a short feature label in bold followed by a colon, then one or two sentences, 200 characters maximum. Order bullets by what the shopper checks first: size and fit, material, use, care, what is included.

Description Paragraph

One paragraph that places the product in the shopper's life. It can repeat the most important bullet fact in different words, but it should not restate every bullet.

Measurements and Units

US customary units first, metric in parentheses when the attribute data has both: "22 in (56 cm)." Never convert units the GPT was not given; if only metric is available, use metric and flag it for the reviewer. Dimensions are written as width x depth x height.

Alt Text

One sentence, under 125 characters, describing what the image shows and anything the shopper needs from it (color, scale, a feature in use). Do not start with "image of" or "picture of." See KB-07.

5. Word Lists

Banned Words (voice reasons)

These are banned because they are vague or sound like advertising, not because they are illegal: best-in-class, game-changer, revolutionary, must-have, elevate, unleash, boasts, luxurious, stunning, perfect, amazing, ultimate, world-class, next-level, curated.

Banned Words (compliance reasons)

These are listed here so the writer sees them early, but the rule lives in KB-09: eco-friendly, green, sustainable, non-toxic, hypoallergenic, antibacterial, guaranteed, lifetime, #1, best-selling, clinically proven, Made in USA.

Preferred Swaps

Instead of Write Why
spacious the actual capacity or dimensions Specific over impressive
high-quality the material and construction detail Quality has to be shown, not claimed
easy to clean "wipes clean with a damp cloth" or "dishwasher safe" Only if the care attribute says so
durable the material and what it resists "Durable" alone invites a substantiation question
perfect for any room one or two specific rooms or uses Nothing is perfect for any room

6. Category Notes

Home goods. Always include care instructions if they are in the attributes. Color names should match the listing exactly; do not rename "Sage" to "Soft Green."

Furniture. Dimensions go in the first bullet. If assembly is required, say so plainly and give the time estimate only if it is in the data. Weight capacity is never estimated.

Kitchen. State the material that touches food. Heat limits, oven safety, and dishwasher compatibility only when the attributes support them; food-safety language goes to KB-09.

Apparel accessories. Sizing first. Leather, metal, and fabric claims must match the material field word for word; "genuine leather" and "vegan leather" are different products.

7. When Information Is Missing

The GPT should never fill a gap with a guess. If an attribute a shopper would need is missing (dimensions on furniture, material on anything that touches food, sizing on accessories), the draft includes a bracketed note such as [MISSING: item_dimensions] and the reviewer gets the draft back with that note at the top. A description with a visible hole is a better outcome than one with an invented number.
